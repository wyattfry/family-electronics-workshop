# Unit 0: The Workshop

Set up one bench the whole family shares, and agree on the rules before anyone heats an iron.

## The rules (print these and post them at the bench)

1. **The iron lives in its stand.** It's in your hand or in the stand, never on the bench.
2. **Safety glasses on** whenever anyone is soldering or clipping leads. Clipped leads fly
   at eyes.
3. **Hold the handle, never the metal.** Assume every tip, joint and part is hot.
4. **Fan on** while soldering. Flux smoke goes away from faces.
5. **Wash hands** after soldering and before eating. No food or drinks at the bench.
6. **Power off before touching a circuit.** Batteries go in last and come out first.
7. **Say "hot!"** when passing anything that's been near the iron.
8. **The parent handles**: bare lithium cells, etchant chemicals, mains power, and anything
   with a blade.

**Burn first aid:** cool running water for 10 or more minutes. No ice, no butter. Anything
bigger than a small spot gets checked by a grown-up.

## Bench layout

- **Back of the bench:** fume fan (pulling smoke *away* from faces), lamp or magnifier,
  power supply.
- **Middle:** a silicone mat as the work area, with helping hands.
- **The kid's dominant-hand side:** the iron in its stand, plus the brass tip cleaner.
  The cord never crosses the work.
- **The other side:** parts tray, multimeter, cutters and pliers.

### Lead or lead-free?

- **Leaded solder (63/37)** flows more easily and is more forgiving for beginners.
- **Lead-free** needs about 30–40 °C more heat and makes duller joints.

Lead exposure from hobby soldering comes mostly from hands to mouth, not from the fumes.
Either choice works with strict handwashing. We use **lead-free at the kids' station**
anyway, so that mistakes around hygiene cost nothing.

**Tip temperatures:**

| Solder | Tip temperature |
|---|---|
| Leaded | about 330 °C |
| Lead-free | about 360–370 °C |

## Roles by age

- **8 y.o.:**
  - Solders with a parent right beside them for the first few sessions, with a hand on the
    board rather than on the iron.
  - Owns the part-sorting, testing with the multimeter and "quality inspector" jobs.
- **11 y.o.:**
  - Has soldered before. Works independently on through-hole after one supervised
    check-off (see the [skill ladder](skill-ladder.md)).
  - Mentors the 8 y.o. Teaching is how you really learn it.
